How a case works

A story and a case file, bound into one book

  1. Take the job

    Someone who has run out of time sends for you. The opening letter says who's paying, and why it has to be an outsider.

  2. Read the paper

    Every chapter hands you a document that looks like the real thing: a payroll, a telegram, a pressing-plant manifest, a union contract.

  3. Strike the names

    Each paper proves some people on the master sheet can't be the one. Strike them in pencil, with a ruler.

  4. Check your count

    Compare your count and your line-number sum with the narrator's. When one line is left, you've reached the only conclusion.

The house rules

What every case promises

Each book is set in a real moment of history, told through paperwork built to look and read like the real thing. The rules don't change from book to book.

  • No murderEvery case is about a person the record erased. Nobody is killed for a plot twist.
  • One provable answerNo guessing, no trick ending. Everything you need is on the page, and every step can be checked.
  • Real historyA real storm, a real music town, a real wartime secret. The people and papers are invented. The history isn't.
  • Hints in three tiersA nudge, then the rule, then a shove. They're numbered out of order, so you can't read ahead by accident.
  • The proof, sealedThe complete solution waits at the back behind a sealed page, for when you've signed your last count.
  • A bonus caseEvery book ends with Case Zero, a short case that opens the next story.

Questions

Before you sit down at the desk

Do I need to be good at puzzles or history?

No. The first stage of every book shows you exactly how to strike, line by line. After that, each chapter asks what the paper proves, and the hints at the back catch you if you stall.

What do I need?

A pencil, not a pen, and something with a straight edge. Everything else is in the book.

Is it a novel or a puzzle book?

Both. It reads like a short novel told in the second person, and every chapter hands you evidence to work. The story moves when you do.

Do I have to read them in order?

No. Each case stands on its own. Reading in order gives you a small bonus: the people you help in one book send for you in the next.

Can two people solve it together?

That's how it's built. Every stage is checked by two counts and two sums. One of you reads, one of you strikes, then you compare. It makes a good gift for a couple or a parent and grown child.

Is there violence?

No murder, ever. ALL HANDS is set around a real shipwreck, and it treats the men who were lost with care. The other cases are set in a recording studio, a tool shop and a film vault.

I'm stuck. What now?

Turn to STUCK? at the back. Each stage has three hints: a nudge, the rule, and a shove. If you're still stuck, the full proof is sealed behind the last pages.
